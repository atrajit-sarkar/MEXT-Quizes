Based on the MEXT English question papers from 2015 to 2019 provided in the sources, the exam pattern is highly standardized. Whether you are applying as an **Undergraduate** or a **Research Student**, the English exam follows the exact same structure.

Here is the blueprint to generate your mock exams.

### **MEXT English Exam Structure**

*   **Total Time:** 60 Minutes
*   **Total Questions:** 50 Questions
*   **Format:** All questions are Multiple Choice (4 options: A, B, C, D)
*   **Pacing:** You have approximately **1.2 minutes per question**, making time management the biggest challenge.

---

### **Detailed Section Breakdown**

The exam consistently consists of **5 Sections (I–V)**. When creating your mock, ensure you include the following types of questions in this specific order:

#### **Section I: Vocabulary in Context (10 Questions)**
*   **Task:** Choose the correct word to complete the sentence.
*   **Focus:** Nuance, collocations, and definitions. The options often feature words that look similar or belong to the same field but have different specific meanings.
*   **Examples from the sources:**
    *   Distinguishing between *diagnoses, medicines, patients, and symptoms*.
    *   Distinguishing between *disengaged, disgruntled, disinterested, and disposed*.
    *   Understanding phrasal verbs or idioms like "in the pink" vs. "under the weather".

#### **Section II: Grammar & Phrasing (10 Questions)**
*   **Task:** Choose the correct grammatical structure or phrase to complete the sentence.
*   **Focus:** Verb tenses, prepositions, conjunctions, and relative clauses.
*   **Examples from the sources:**
    *   **Prepositions/Phrases:** "My PC is lighter than yours **by** 100 grams".
    *   **Tenses/Conditionals:** "If Alice **had known** Doug’s flight was going to be...".
    *   **Verbs:** Usage of *lying* vs. *laying* ("mind **my lying** on the sofa").

#### **Section III: Error Identification (10 Questions)**
*   **Task:** You are given a short paragraph or sentence with four underlined sections (A, B, C, D). You must identify which one is **grammatically incorrect**.
*   **Focus:** Subject-verb agreement, article usage, wrong word forms, or syntax errors.
*   **Examples from the sources:**
    *   *2019 Exam:* A sentence about the 1918 flu: "the virus itself did not **caused** all of the deaths" (Error is "caused"; should be "cause").
    *   *2015 Exam:* "Many of us see planes as... virus around a long metal tube" (Checking article usage or plurality).

#### **Section IV: Cloze Test / Fill in the Blanks (10 Questions)**
*   **Task:** A single long passage (approx. 300–400 words) with 10 numbered blanks. You must select the best word or phrase to fill each blank.
*   **Focus:** Context clues, linking words, and narrative flow.
*   **Themes observed in past papers:**
    *   *2019:* Biography of Marcel Proust and his novel *In Search of Lost Time*.
    *   *2018:* The rules of English adjective order (opinion-size-age-shape...).
    *   *2017:* The etymology and evolution of the word "cool".
    *   *2016:* World population growth history.

#### **Section V: Reading Comprehension (10 Questions)**
*   **Task:** Two separate reading passages (Part I and Part II), each followed by 5 multiple-choice questions.
*   **Focus:** Detail retrieval, inference, and understanding the author's main point.
*   **Passage Length:** Typically 400–600 words per passage.
*   **Topic Variety:**
    *   **Linguistics/Culture:** Endangered languages, Language and thought (Snow words).
    *   **Science/Health:** Sugar-free drinks and tooth enamel, Food waste statistics, Louis Pasteur and chirality.
    *   **History/Sociology:** The history of spears, Organic food fraud.

### **How to Create Your Mock Exam**

To accurately test your preparation, assemble a test paper using questions from different years or similar practice materials using the following template:

| Section | Question Count | Topic Suggestions for Mocks |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **I. Vocab** | 10 | Mix advanced adjectives (e.g., *keenly, inadvertently*) with phrasal verbs. |
| **II. Grammar** | 10 | Focus on conditionals (*if/had*), passive voice, and prepositions (*at, by, in*). |
| **III. Error ID** | 10 | Use sentences 2–3 lines long. Introduce errors in verb tense or singular/plural agreement. |
| **IV. Cloze** | 10 (1 Passage) | Find a Wikipedia article or essay on a generic academic topic (e.g., "The History of Tea") and remove 10 connecting words/verbs. |
| **V. Reading** | 10 (2 Passages) | **Passage A:** A scientific report (e.g., Climate Change or Nutrition).<br>**Passage B:** A humanities essay (e.g., Art History or Anthropology). |

**Strict Simulation Rules:**
1.  **Set a timer for 60 minutes.** Do not go over.
2.  **No dictionary allowed.**
3.  **Answer order:** Many students find it efficient to do **Section I & II** first (speed), then **Section V** (requires focus), and save **Section III & IV** for the remaining time.
